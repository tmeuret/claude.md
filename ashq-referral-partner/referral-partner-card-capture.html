Referral Partner Card Capture

Scan a business card, add one personal detail, and a tailored "nice to meet you" email with your coffee calendar link goes out for you. Built in GoHighLevel for every agent's sub-account.

From card to coffee in about a minute

Donna Sanders in Houston asked for this, and it works for every agent on the team. You meet a CPA, a dentist, or the activities director at a senior community. You scan the card before you leave the parking lot. The AI writes the follow-up, and it goes out with your coffee calendar link.

  1. You · 10 secScan the cardLeadConnector app, Scan Business Card. AI reads name, email, phone, and company, and saves the card image to the contact.
  2. GHL · instantText with a linkTag rp-card fires a workflow that texts you the Nice-to-Meet-You form link.
  3. You · 45 secAdd the human partPartner type, where you met, the personal touch ("loves Crown Royal"), what you talked about.
  4. AI · secondsWrite, then proofreadOne AI step writes the email. A second checks name spelling, typos, and placeholders.
  5. GHL · 10 minSend or holdClean drafts send after a 10-minute window. Flagged drafts come to you for a quick fix.
  6. PartnerBooks coffeeYour coffee calendar link sits right under the note. Booking tags them rp-coffee-booked.

Training mode for the first 3

Every new agent starts with rp_auto_send = no. The first two or three drafts come to you to read and approve. When you like what you see, flip it to yes.

Auto mode after that

Emails go out on their own unless the proofreader finds a problem: a misspelled name, a typo, leftover placeholder text, or a missing email. Only those come back to you.

Two separate referral engines

This is the B2B engine for professional referral partners. Client referrals (happy clients sending friends and family) run on their own track and never mix with this list.

Agent Quick Start

This is the whole routine once your sub-account is set up. Keep this tab on your phone for your first event.

Before your first event · 10 minutes, once
  1. Install the LeadConnector app (App Store or Google Play) and log in to your sub-account. Update it: the scanner needs version 3.74 or later.
  2. Connect your Google or Outlook calendar to your Coffee calendar so partners only see times you are free.
  3. Check your four settings with Tim or George: your first name, your coffee calendar link, your email signature, and rp_auto_send (starts at no).
  4. Scan your own business card once as a test. You should get the text within a minute.
At the event · about 1 minute per card
  1. Open LeadConnector, go to Contacts, tap the blue +, then Scan Business Card. Scan the front, and the back if it has info.
  2. Check the first name and email. Fix anything the scanner misread. The first name is the one the email will use.
  3. Add the tag rp-card and switch on Add to Files & Folders so the card image stays on the contact. Tap Save.
  4. Tap the link in the text you get and fill in the Nice-to-Meet-You form: partner type, where you met, the personal touch, and what you talked about. Submit.

The personal touch makes the email

Specific beats nice. The AI only uses what you give it.

  • Good Loves Crown Royal. Says Apple is overrated.
  • Good Daughter just started at Texas A&M. Proud Aggie dad.
  • Good Runs the Bayou City 10K every March.
  • Too thin Nice guy. Good conversation.

Leave out health, religion, politics, money trouble, and family trouble. The AI is told to skip those anyway.

When a draft comes back to you

  1. You get a text and an email that say Draft ready, with the reason it was held.
  2. Open the contact. Read the RP Email Draft field and edit it right there if needed.
  3. Add the tag rp-approved. It sends within a minute, with your edits.

Don't want it sent at all? Add rp-cancel instead.

No signal or scanner trouble? Open the Nice-to-Meet-You form from your bookmarks, type the name and email yourself, and attach a photo of the card. The rest of the process is the same.

Part 3 · Coffee calendar
  1. Calendars, New Calendar, Personal Booking (one agent). Name it Coffee with [Agent]. Set 30 minutes, a 15-minute buffer, and 24-hour minimum notice.
  2. Location: "Coffee shop of your choice, I'll confirm by text", or the agent's regular spot. Donna picks her Houston spot.
  3. Form fields: name, email, phone, and one optional question, "Anything you'd like me to bring?"
  4. Copy the booking link into rp_coffee_calendar_link.
Part 4 · Nice-to-Meet-You form
  1. Sites, Forms, Builder, new form RP – Nice to Meet You. Make it mobile-first and single-column.
  2. Fields in this order: First Name, Last Name, Email (required), Phone, Company Name, RP Partner Type (required), RP Where We Met, RP Personal Touch (required), RP Conversation Notes, and Card Photo (File Upload, JPG/PNG/HEIC, optional).
  3. Help text under Personal Touch: "Be specific. 'Loves Crown Royal' beats 'nice guy.' Skip health, religion, and politics."
  4. On-submit message: "Got it. Your note is being written."
  5. Check the sub-account's Allow Duplicate Contact setting is off, with email as the primary match. That way the form updates the contact the scanner created instead of making a second one.
  6. Copy the form's share link into rp_details_form_link.
Part 5 · Workflow 1: RP 1 – Card Scanned
  1. Trigger: Contact Tag, tag added = rp-card.
  2. Internal Notification (SMS to the assigned user; for a one-agent sub-account, pick the agent):
    Got {{contact.first_name}}'s card. 45 seconds to personalize: {{custom_values.rp_details_form_link}}?email={{contact.email}}&first_name={{contact.first_name}}

The ?email= part pre-fills the form. If the form doesn't pre-fill in your account, the link still works and the agent types the email.

Part 6 · Workflow 2: RP 2 – Write & Send
  1. Trigger: Form Submitted, form = RP – Nice to Meet You. Workflow setting: allow re-entry off.
  2. Add Tag: rp-partner. Remove tag rp-card.
  3. GPT powered by OpenAI, action type Custom. Model: GPT 5 Mini, temperature 0.7. Paste the Email Writer prompt from the AI Prompts tab.
  4. Update Contact Field: RP Email Draft = the GPT output. Pick it from the merge-field picker. It usually reads {{chatgpt.1.response}}, but the number depends on the step position.
  5. GPT powered by OpenAI, Custom, temperature 0.1. Paste the Proofreader prompt.
  6. Update Contact Field: RP QA Result = the second GPT output.
  7. If/Else: RP QA Result is SEND.
    • Yes branch: Wait 10 minutes. Then If/Else: has tag rp-cancel? If yes, end. If no, Send Email (template below) and Add Tag rp-intro-sent.
    • No branch: Add Tag rp-needs-review. Internal Notification by SMS and email to the agent: Draft ready for {{contact.first_name}} ({{contact.rp_qa_result}}). Open the contact, check RP Email Draft, then add tag rp-approved. Then Wait for the event "Contact Tag Added" = rp-approved, timeout 48 hours. Then If/Else on tag rp-approved: if yes, Send Email, add rp-intro-sent, and remove rp-needs-review. If no, send a reminder notification.

Part 8 · Test, then push to the team
  1. In the template account, scan a real card (use your own), fill in the form, and confirm the draft is held with REVIEW: training mode.
  2. Change rp_auto_send to yes and run it again. The email should send after 10 minutes.
  3. Test a bad one on purpose: type the first name as "Mrak" on the card and "Mark" in the notes. The proofreader should hold it.
  4. From the agency view, go to Account Snapshots. Refresh the snapshot and click Push Updates. Select the agent sub-accounts, then choose Custom Fields, Custom Values, Forms, Calendars, and Workflows.
  5. In each agent's sub-account, set their five custom values and connect their calendar. Keep rp_auto_send = no until they have approved 3 drafts.
Heads up on pushes. A snapshot push overwrites the workflows and forms it carries, so any edits an agent made to them are lost. Custom values that already exist are left alone, so their name, calendar, and signature stay safe.

AI Prompts

Paste these into the two GPT powered by OpenAI steps in Workflow 2. The merge fields fill in automatically for each contact.

Email Writer · step 3 · temperature 0.7

You are writing a short "nice to meet you" email for {{custom_values.rp_agent_first_name}}, an independent Medicare and retirement agent with The Retirement Answer Team. The reader is a professional the agent just met in person and hopes to build a referral relationship with. The reader is NOT a Medicare client.

FACTS (use only these, never invent anything):
- Their first name: {{contact.first_name}}
- Their company: {{contact.company_name}}
- Partner type: {{contact.rp_partner_type}}
- Where we met: {{contact.rp_where_met}}
- Personal touch: {{contact.rp_personal_touch}}
- What we talked about: {{contact.rp_conversation_notes}}

WRITE, in this order:
1. Greeting: their first name and a comma, alone on the first line.
2. One sentence naming where you met.
3. One or two sentences that use the personal touch warmly and naturally. Light humor is welcome if it fits. Mention it once. Add no details that are not in the facts.
4. One or two sentences on how the agent helps this partner type's clients or patients (see the angles below). Say "your clients" or "your patients."
5. One sentence inviting them to coffee. Do not include a link; the booking button is added below your text.
6. End with "With kind regards," on its own line. No name and no signature.

ANGLE BY PARTNER TYPE:
- Doctor (PCP or Specialist): patients turning 65 or changing plans who want to keep their doctors; the agent checks networks before anyone enrolls.
- Dentist: patients surprised that Original Medicare does not cover routine dental care; the agent walks them through their options.
- Optometrist: patients surprised that Original Medicare does not cover routine eye exams or glasses; the agent walks them through their options.
- CPA / Tax Preparer: clients retiring or turning 65; IRMAA surcharges; HSA contributions that have to stop before Medicare starts.
- Financial Advisor: healthcare costs inside the retirement plan; IRMAA; timing the move off employer coverage.
- Estate / Elder Law Attorney: clients planning for long-term care, or who may qualify for Medicaid or a Medicare Savings Program.
- Home Health / Senior Care: clients whose plan decides which agencies and services they can use.
- Senior Center: free, no-sales education sessions such as Medicare 101 and "Stop the Calls."
- Senior Community: residents facing plan changes or non-renewals; on-site education; a move can open a Special Enrollment Period.
- Other: a trusted, local person their clients can call with Medicare questions.

RULES:
- 60 to 120 words. Warm, direct, peer to peer.
- Write in first person as the agent ("I").
- Never name a Medicare plan, carrier, premium, or benefit amount.
- If the personal touch involves health, religion, politics, money trouble, or family trouble, leave it out.
- No em dashes. At most one exclamation point.
- Never use: "I hope this email finds you well," "just wanted to," "touch base," "circle back," "delve," "leverage," "synergy," "act now."
- Output only the email body as plain text. No subject line, no quotation marks, no notes.

Proofreader · step 5 · temperature 0.1

You are a strict proofreader for outgoing email. Answer with exactly one line and nothing else.

AUTO_SEND setting: {{custom_values.rp_auto_send}}
Contact first name from the card: {{contact.first_name}}
Contact email: {{contact.email}}
Draft:
"""
{{chatgpt.1.response}}
"""

Check in this order:
1. If AUTO_SEND is not exactly "yes", answer: REVIEW: training mode
2. The greeting uses the contact first name spelled exactly as above.
3. No misspelled words, typos, or grammar mistakes.
4. No leftover placeholders or labels such as {{ }}, [ ], "Personal touch:", "N/A", or "undefined".
5. No Medicare plan, carrier, premium, or benefit amount is named.
6. Nothing about health, religion, politics, money trouble, or family trouble.
7. Between 50 and 140 words and ends with "With kind regards,".
8. The contact email is present and looks like a real address.

If every check passes, answer exactly: SEND
Otherwise answer: REVIEW: followed by the first problem in 12 words or fewer.
In the proofreader, replace {{chatgpt.1.response}} with whatever the merge picker shows for the Email Writer step's output in your workflow.

Team AI Use Case: the card that doesn't die in your cup holder

Every one of you has a stack of business cards in the car. The dentist from the health fair. The CPA from the Chamber breakfast. The activities director who said "call me about a presentation." Most of those cards never get a follow-up, and not because you don't care. By the time you're home, there's a client call, a carrier training, and dinner.

Donna asked for a better way, and she asked for it in Houston, where the drive between meetings eats the day. So we built one.

What the AI does

  • Reads the card: name, email, phone, company.
  • Writes a 60-to-120-word note that sounds like a person, using the detail you gave it.
  • Proofreads its own work and holds anything with a misspelled name or a typo.
  • Puts your coffee calendar right under the note.

What you do

  • Notice something real about the person. That is the job, and AI can't do it for you.
  • Spend 45 seconds typing it in.
  • Read your first three drafts before they go out.
  • Show up for coffee.

What it costs

  • Card scans: 50 free per sub-account per day, then $0.02 each.
  • AI writing and proofreading: billed through GHL at OpenAI's token rates. A short email costs pennies or less.
  • Your time: about a minute per card, down from the follow-up that never happened.

Be the agent, not the office assistant. Your time belongs in front of beneficiaries and the professionals who send them to you. Typing contact info into a CRM at 9 p.m. is an ankle-biter. Hand it off.

Tell the AI "go do this." Don't ask it "how do I do this." The prompts on this page are instructions, not questions. That shift is where the 10x comes from. You still check its work, which is why training mode exists.

Keep the two referral engines separate. Referral partners (CPAs, advisors, attorneys, doctors, dentists, senior communities) are one pillar. Clients who send their friends are another. This page builds the first one.

Rollout
  1. This week: Tim builds the template account and pushes the snapshot. Donna goes first in Houston.
  2. Before your next event: install LeadConnector, connect your coffee calendar, and scan your own card as a test.
  3. First three cards: read every draft and approve it. Tell Tim what you changed so the prompt gets better for everyone.
  4. After that: auto mode. You only see the drafts the proofreader flags.
  5. Monday team call: share one coffee that came from a card. Wins belong to the agent who earned them.
